---
title: "Whistleblower Disclosure Leads to Improved Access to Inpatient Mental
  Health Care at Missouri VA Hospital "
date: 2026-11-02
tags:
  - disclosure of wrongdoing
---
WASHINGTON, D.C./November 2, 2026 — Today, the U.S. Office of Special Counsel (OSC) notified the President and Congress of the results of a U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) investigation into practices on the acute psychiatric care unit at the Harry S. Truman Memorial Veterans' Hospital in Columbia, Missouri. 

A whistleblower raised concerns about how patients were admitted to the unit. The VA Office of the Medical Inspector conducted an on-site investigation in June 2025. The investigation did not substantiate the specific concerns raised, but it uncovered a different issue: the unit's local admission procedures were stricter than what national VA policy allows. Under Veterans Health Administration Directive 1160.06, inpatient mental health care must be available to all eligible veterans who need hospital-level care for a mental health condition. 

Investigators also found that some staff mistakenly believed they were authorized to override a provider's decision to admit a patient when assessing how a new patient might affect the unit's environment. This created a risk that veterans who qualified for care could face delays or be turned away. The report also noted that limited training on caring for patients with complex needs, such as dementia, may have made staff reluctant to accept those patients. 

In response, the VA adopted three recommendations: revise the unit's procedures to comply with national policy, train staff on the updated process and nurses' role in admissions, and provide clinical training on caring for patients with complex needs. The hospital confirmed that all three have been carried out. The revised procedures were published on July 18, 2025, and the VA's national director for inpatient mental health services found that they comply with national policy.

"We thank the whistleblower for coming forward, "said Special Counsel Charles Baldis. "While the investigation did not substantiate the specific concerns raised, the disclosure revealed a gap between local procedures and national access standards. It also led to meaningful corrective actions that better ensure eligible veterans can receive the inpatient care they need, and that staff are better prepared for complex clinical situations. It is a reminder that whistleblower disclosures can lead to improvements in unexpected ways."
